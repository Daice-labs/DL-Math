# Eliminating paired guards from shuffled cubic parity clauses

**Construction theorem.** Let $H$ be a finite connected simple cubic graph on $n$ vertices. Pair its vertices arbitrarily. Give every graph edge its own bit and every vertex pair a separate guard bit. At each vertex encode the two complementary charged parities with the two local 3-CNF constructions below, with fresh helpers. The input may undergo any dense variable renaming, any consistent individual variable complementation, and any permutations of clauses or literals. A uniform algorithm starting from these clauses derives all $n$ guard-affine equations before its global contradiction argument. It decides the input with zero global guard splits. It produces an Extended Frege refutation on odd charge, or a full original assignment on even charge. Both outputs can be checked in polynomial bit work. The refutation uses $O(n\log(n+2))$ lines and $O(n\log^2(n+2))$ bits with shared references.

## Original clauses and exact recovery

At a vertex write $x,y,z$ for the three distinct incident bits, $g$ for the shared guard and $c$ for the charge. The local ordering of the incident bits may vary by vertex.

* On the first arm four clauses define a private $h=x\oplus y$ by forbidding exactly the four wrong triples. Two clauses with a common disjunct $g$ require $h\oplus z=c$ when $g=0$.
* On the other arm forbid each of the four triples with $x\oplus y\oplus z\ne c\oplus1$ when $g=1$. For its three forbidding literals $l_x,l_y,l_z$, use a distinct private $s$ and emit $(\neg g\lor l_x\lor s)$ and $(\neg s\lor l_y\lor l_z)$. Resolving the pair gives the intended four-literal prohibition. Conversely, a true four-literal prohibition can always be extended to $s$. If the first ordinary disjunction is false, set $s=1$. If the second is false, set $s=0$. They cannot both be false.

There are exactly $7n$ original variables and $14n$ ternary clauses. Only the split variables have physical occurrence count two. Their signs are opposite. Resolve them and group the resulting four-literal rows by their variable sets. No two vertices have the same group, since two vertices of a simple graph share at most one graph edge. Each group contains four different rows. Only the guard keeps one literal sign throughout the four rows. Each of the other three coordinates attains both values among the four forbidden triples.

Find the intact arm by its two rows containing the opposite guard literal, one of the three edge variables, and a private $h$. A four-row parity block on $h$ and the other two edge variables identifies the correct pair uniquely. Even at paired adjacent vertices a foreign pair could fit only if both vertices shared all three graph edges. The producer verifies the four parity rows and the full local relation, disjointness of all private roles, exactly two users per physical edge and guard, the simple connected cubic incidence graph, and use of every original clause. Occurrences and incidences survive all allowed permutations. The falsifying signs of the actual rows give the right charge after physical complementation.

Writing $b_v$ for the charge at physical $g=0$, the exact projected relation at vertex $v$ is

$$
x\oplus y\oplus z\oplus g=b_v.
$$

The general two-arm interpolation identity specializes here to slope one. In proof production, resolving each of the four split pairs supplies one half of the eight clauses of this equation. Resolving each intact XOR row with the guarded row of opposite $h$-polarity supplies the other half. The constructor checks that the eight rows are exactly the eight forbidden four-bit words. Three fresh acyclic XOR definitions name the successive parities $x\oplus y$, $x\oplus y\oplus z$, and $E_v=x\oplus y\oplus z\oplus g$. Constant-size propositional deductions give one signed unit for $E_v=b_v$. Each of these units covers both settings of the guard. All local units are available before the global proof begins.

## Global decision and original SAT lift

Every graph edge and every guard occurs twice in the sum over vertices. Thus odd total charge implies a contradiction. On even charge, **every prescribed full guard vector** extends to a solution. Move its chosen guard value to each vertex's right-hand side. The adjusted charges still sum to zero. Set non-tree edges to zero. Process a spanning tree from its leaves inward, assigning each parent edge the residual charge of its child and updating the parent's charge. The root has residual zero. The implementation uses the all-zero physical guard vector. Each original private XOR bit and each private split bit can now be chosen locally. They occur in just one vertex's clauses. The producer writes the full original-variable assignment and the fresh model checker evaluates the actual source.

If an entire vertex equation is released on the negative, set its right-hand side to the value that makes the total even. The tree argument supplies values satisfying the other equations. This explains why a local contradiction is insufficient even though each local rule has constant size.

## An explicit Frege proof of cancellation

Use a balanced binary tree with one leaf per physical graph bit or guard. Start with every leaf equal to a defined false bit $Z$. Visit each vertex with its four actual bit names. At the first occurrence of a bit $x$, change its leaf from $Z$ to $x$. At the second, change it back to $Z$. Each occurrence changes only one rootward path. A changed child's old and new values differ by $x$. With one identical sibling on both sides, its two parents also differ by $x$. The three premise relations imply the desired XOR relation between the old parent, $x$ and the new parent. The construction derives its actual clauses from those for the two parent definitions and the changed child. Repeated physical identities are simplified as literals. These implications use at most six distinct Boolean atoms.

For four consecutive updates, compose their root relations using the three local XOR definitions of $E_v$. This yields the checked relation between the preceding root, $E_v$, and the new root. The proved unit for $E_v$ and the preceding root unit give the new signed root unit. After the last vertex all leaves have returned to $Z$ and the all-zero subtree is represented by $Z$ itself. Odd charge has proved $Z$ against $\neg Z$. The terminal Frege formula is $\neg\mathsf{True}$. The file contains explicit implications and modus ponens steps. Gaussian elimination, graph recognition, summation and XOR cancellation are never verifier inference rules.

The local constructor can prove an entailed clause by splitting only on the still-unassigned atoms of its displayed premises. At a leaf it uses a falsified premise, then resolves opposite cases and weakens to the required clause. At most seven atoms occur in any such entailment in the named constructor. An already proved clause can be reused. Every weakening or resolution is emitted as Hilbert lines using disjunction injection, disjunction elimination, composition and cut. The extension rule checks freshness and permits only prior variables in its defining formula. The fixed twelve auxiliary Frege schemata are proved once, without hypotheses, in a fixed finite Hilbert calculus. The fresh checker replays that library, verifies each actual schema instance syntactically, and checks every original clause reference, extension, MP and the terminal formula. A customary finite Hilbert basis augmented by finitely many proved fixed schemata is again an EF presentation. The main file uses that presentation, with syntax nodes interned to preserve identical subformulas.

## Work on the growing family

There are $2n$ public variables and $4n$ total occurrences in the recovered equations. Every physical clause block has constant arity. Each of the $4n$ leaf updates visits at most $\lceil\log_2(2n)\rceil$ ancestors. Each ancestor, vertex, source clause, and bounded local proof case contributes at most a constant number of bounded-size Frege lines. The total is $O(n\log(n+2))$ lines, including fresh definitions and shared references. Instantiating each fixed library proof in full would multiply the line bound by a constant. Variable IDs and proof references cost $O(\log(n+2))$ bits each. The serialized proof and syntax tables therefore require $O(n\log^2(n+2))$ bits. The model uses $O(n\log(n+2))$ bits.

Recognition indexes signed occurrences, helper resolvents and three-variable blocks. A graph traversal suffices for the SAT tree. Let $K=O(n\log(n+2))$ bound the fixed-arity records of construction and verification. Local truth checks have an absolute constant number of variables. Even charging the prototype's repeated copying of its growing assignment dictionaries on SAT gives $O(n^2+K)$ operations on indexed records before dictionary lookup and encoding costs. Keys can be compared deterministically by scans. A full scan of the records with input-sized identifiers costs polynomial bit work. With $L$ actual source bits, $n=O(L+1)$, so reading the source, building and emitting the proof, and freshly reading and checking its evidence all take polynomial total bit work. The fresh checker receives the proof or model and original clauses. The argument never branches on a vector of guard values.

Polynomial proofs of ordinary Tseitin contradictions are established in the literature. This result supplies a direct source-recognition and EF-construction chain for the **specified two-arm hidden encoding**. It gives no bound on the number of surviving branches for arbitrary 3-CNF or for arms outside this grammar.
